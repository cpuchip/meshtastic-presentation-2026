A pocket radio that texts your family for miles.
No tower. No subscription. No license. No monthly bill.

What it is, in one minute

A small radio, a free app, and your phone. It sends text and location, not voice and not internet. It runs for days on a charge.

Every radio repeats what it hears. Your message hops from node to node, so you do not need to reach your neighbor directly. You just need to reach somebody. That is the mesh.

The one hardware rule

Screw the antenna on BEFORE you power it up. Transmitting with no antenna can damage the radio. Everything else is forgiving.

Setup, start to finish

  1. Charge it.
  2. Update it: drag-and-drop (T1000-E) or flasher.meshtastic.org in Chrome (Heltec). Do not tick "full erase." It wipes your radio's identity keys, and your private messages to everyone who already knows you stop working until you both delete each other and start over. A normal update keeps them.
  3. Install the free Meshtastic app (iOS or Android) and pair over Bluetooth.
  4. Check two settings: Region = US, and Preset = LongFast. Wrong region and it will not transmit at all. Wrong preset and it transmits into an empty room. Both look exactly like a dead radio. These two are the first things to check, always.
  5. Name your node something you will recognize. You are now on the mesh.

Channels: what to set, and what not to touch Turn this page over for what to buy
There are two separate questions, and mixing them up causes almost every problem.
1. Can our radios hear each other? That is region + preset + frequency.
2. Can we read what we hear? That is the channel name and its key.
Same room, different language: you hear nothing useful. Different room: nothing helps. Get the radio settings right first.

Renaming the main channel moves your frequency

Renaming your main channel changes your frequency. Meshtastic takes a hash of your primary channel's name and uses it to pick the actual frequency slot. Rename it and your radio quietly moves off the frequency your neighbors are on. Not a different chat room. A different frequency. No key will fix it.

So if you want privacy, do not rename your main channel. Read on.

How to get a private family channel (the right way)

  1. Leave the main channel exactly as it came. No name, default key, LongFast, region US. That is what keeps you on the mesh with everybody else.
  2. Add a second channel. Give it a name and generate a random key. (Not "default" and not the "simple" ones. Those are published and everyone has them.)
  3. Share it by QR code to the phones you trust. That is the whole handshake.

Now you have both: the public channel for reach, and a private channel only your family can read. You get 8 channels total, so there is room.

Careful: that QR is the key. Anyone who photographs it is in. Do not put it on a slide or a Facebook post.

"Is LongFast private?" No.

LongFast is the channel every radio ships with, and its key is published in the documentation. Everyone on earth has it. Treat it like the public square: good for hello, for coordinating, for finding people. Wrong for anything you would not say out loud at the expo.

Keep it anyway. It is how you find your neighbors, and how they find you.

Do my messages still get through if we are not on the same channel?

Yes, and this is the best part. A radio that cannot read your message still carries it. The address on the outside is readable; only the contents are locked. So strangers' nodes pass your private family messages along without ever being able to read a word of them.

Two things follow:

  • Your private channel gets the range of everybody's radios.
  • Owning a node is a contribution. Yours is carrying messages for people you have never met, right now, sitting in a window.

Leave the rebroadcast setting on ALL (the default) and your role on CLIENT. Do not set yours to Router or Repeater because it sounds stronger. Those are for nodes up high with real power, and a badly placed one makes the mesh worse.

Is anyone out here?

Yes. Our regional network is LZMesh (Southwest Missouri), running around 60 nodes on a given day. Map and Discord QR codes are on the front.

map.lzmesh.com · discord.lzarc.com

Good news: they do it the way we just told you to. LZMesh leaves the default channel alone and adds theirs as a second channel. So your brand-new radio is already on their frequency and already hears them. Add their channel from their site when you want to read that traffic too.

Two cautions. Scanning their channel QR can replace channels you already have, so save a private one first. And if your node list looks empty, check region and preset before anything else. A brand-new board here came up on LongTurbo instead of LongFast: that put it on 908.750 MHz while everything else sat on 906.875. It found nobody, on the same table as three working radios.

When you want more

  • MeshCore: same radios, planned repeaters instead of everyone-repeats. Tighter at scale, more setup. meshcore.co.uk
  • Reticulum: a whole encrypted network stack over any radio. The deep end. reticulum.network
  • Ham radio: voice, real power, repeaters. The Technician license is a weekend of study. Study free at hamstudy.org, built by our friend Richard Bateman (KD7BBC). The FCC charges $35 for the license itself. Studying costs nothing.
    Meshtastic is the on-ramp. Ham is the highway. Plenty of us run both.
